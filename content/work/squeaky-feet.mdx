      The illustration was drawn by hand in Procreate. All tone comes from line density, with no gradients or airbrush. ## Overview

Here's Your Perfect Storm treats a storm as a piece of paperwork. The artwork is staged as a 1985 field survey: a bureaucratic document made by someone whose job was to measure, chart and file a lightning strike. The joke and the feeling are the same thing. You can grid it, label it and give it a revision number, but you can't contain it. The title works the same way: a calm, official handoff of something completely out of control.

## Concept

The cover is drawn as Plate 17A, a technical record of a single strike hitting marshy ground. It's laid out on a kraft-paper sheet with an electrical grid diagram over the landscape. Every number on the sheet is meant to hold up if an engineer reads it. The system data uses a real North American distribution spec (13.2 kV line-to-line, 7.62 kV line-to-neutral, 60 Hz three-phase). Ground-potential isolines follow real decay intervals. The strike sits at 39°04'30" N, 104°48'00" W, on Colorado's Palmer Divide, which has the highest cloud-to-ground lightning density in the state. That's a nod to the band's home. The grid reference PG-7A-19.88 became "Palmer Grid" after the fact.

This one is personal. Before design was my full-time work, I spent seven years mapping electrical grid infrastructure. This record let me turn that world of one-line diagrams, survey grids and system specs into something expressive.
