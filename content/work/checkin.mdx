      ## The problem

      Most habit apps put you alone with a streak counter that resets the day you miss. One slip and the app feels like it's scolding you. The group chat this started in worked better, because showing up was something friends saw each other do.

      ## Decisions

      - **The crew comes first, the streak second.** The home screen is your crew's feed, not your stats. Missed days aren't marked, and the all-time count never goes down.
      - **Nudges without guilt.** You can wave at a crewmate who isn't in yet, once a day. The message says "No pressure" and never mentions a streak.
      - **The banner took four rounds.** It started as a flat block of color with a big "who's in today" card that took over phone screens. Patterns and emoji made it feel cheap. A richer surface still had diagonal lines nobody liked. The final version is compact and holographic, mixed from each crew's own color, so the feed leads.
      - **Phones first.** On a phone, "who's in today" folds into one row inside the banner.

      ## How it's built

      Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS v4, on a Supabase data layer with sign-in. The demo data re-seeds relative to now, so the crew is always mid-day. Effects from three different kits were built side by side on one preview page, then merged into a single set of tokens and a motion budget. The weekly crew recap is an honest AI demo: it runs in the browser, with no model and no key.

      ## Checking the work

      I ran the project's 17 UX checks on every screen, at 375px and 1280px, in light and dark mode, then fixed what failed. Focus now returns to the button that opened a sheet, every tap target reaches 44px, nothing is smaller than 12px, and forms explain what's missing instead of greying out their buttons.

      ## What I'd do next

      Real photo uploads in place of the AI placeholder covers, and real waves sent to a crewmate's phone.

Most habit apps are built around a lonely streak counter that punishes you for missing a day. Check-in is built around the crew. Each crew has its own look, a live feed of who checked in today, and small social gestures (reactions, a wave to a crewmate who isn't in yet) that make showing up feel shared rather than tracked.

It started in a group chat where friends posted what they did each day. Check-in gives that a home.

The ring carousel and emoji reactions are adapted from the 100K Club Premium UI Kit (Bencho), the stat panels and holo sheen from an MMO effects kit, and the bottom sheet from Arc. All MIT.
